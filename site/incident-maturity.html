5.3 / Automation Maturity

Earn automation authority through evidence

Adopt the evidence-assistant stage first, then grant execution authority action by action. The presence of an AI model, tool connector or runbook does not demonstrate operational maturity.

Controls outside the model

Test stale evidence, malicious instructions embedded in logs or tickets, connector failures and model unavailability. Enforce tool authorization and parameter limits independently of model output; keep secrets and unnecessary customer data out of prompts and logs. An approval must identify the action and scope, and expire when the relevant conditions change.

Keep an auditable record of evidence, recommendation, approval, execution and verification. A kill switch stops new automated actions; operators still need a procedure to handle in-flight work and restore control. Evaluate these controls continuously, consistent with the NIST AI Risk Management Framework.

Demonstrate business value

Compare equivalent incident exercises, then controlled production adoption, against the runbook-led baseline. Record incident type, severity, traffic and data coverage; a change in incident mix can distort average recovery time.

  • Response speed: time to detect, engage the correct owner, quantify impact and mitigate.
  • Customer recovery: time to restore service and, separately, clear the backlog; affected customer-minutes and deliveries missing their deadline.
  • Action quality: incorrect recommendations, failed actions, rollbacks and operator overrides, with counts and rates per recommendation or execution.
  • Communication: update timeliness, factual accuracy and unsupported-claim corrections.
  • Effort and cost: responder time and incident cost, including automation operation and review effort.

A lower mean time to recovery is insufficient if automation increases customer impact or unsafe actions. Agree pass/stop thresholds before the pilot. Pause autonomous execution on incorrect scope, lost work, conflicting ownership or customer-objective regression; investigate and revalidate before restoring permission.

Service owners and SRE validate technical outcomes; managers and Product/Business approve the cost and customer tradeoff. The architecture consensus board reviews the automation design before adoption, while the Incident Commander retains authority during the incident.
