4.1 / KPIs and Business Outcomes

Measure customer outcomes, then choose the investment

Accepted ingestion measures demand served at the platform boundary. Successful webhook throughput measures confirmed endpoint acceptance. Neither counts retries as new value, and endpoint acceptance does not prove the customer's business workflow completed.

Measurement contract

Known: the scenario targets at least 99.9% ingestion and delivery availability, delivery within seconds under normal conditions, and low tolerance for event loss. It supplies no measured baseline, exact latency threshold, measurement window or contractual exclusions. The definitions below are proposals for agreement with service owners, Product and Business.

  • Identity: an event is unique by tenant and producer event ID; a delivery obligation by tenant, event and destination. Every network attempt has a separate attempt ID. Freeze the destination set at acceptance or record its later resolution explicitly; never silently remove failed obligations from denominators.
  • Time and cohorts: rate = count during a window / elapsed seconds. Operational views use one-minute buckets and five-minute trends; proposed SLO reporting uses a rolling 28 days. Compare tenant traffic classes and customer cohorts, plus region only if deployed. Report per-tenant drilldowns so large tenants cannot hide small-tenant failures.
  • Observation: record durable acceptance, eligibility, attempts, responses and state transitions with correlated IDs and timestamps. Reconcile gateway acceptance receipts against durable ownership. Clock skew, missing records and delayed telemetry are measured; unknown evidence is not marked healthy.
  • Latency cohort: agree a deadline D before enabling a timeliness SLO. Evaluate obligations whose acceptance time + D falls in the window; count those confirmed accepted by that deadline as good. Pending, failed and ambiguous outcomes miss the deadline. Successful-only latency percentiles are diagnostic, not the complete customer outcome.
  • Collection: propose an initial 28-day baseline covering peaks, deployments and a retry/recovery cycle. All rows below start unmeasured. Use synthetic faults where failures are absent; do not wait for a loss incident to validate P1. Keep raw event IDs in access-controlled records rather than metric labels; use bounded outcome/cohort labels and restricted tenant drilldowns. Never log tokens or webhook payloads for these metrics.

Targets, error budgets and alerts

An SLI is the measured ratio; an SLO is its agreed target and window; an SLA is a separate customer contract. Proposed ingestion and delivery budgets remain separate. For a 99.9% event-based objective, allowed bad events = eligible events × 0.001; burn rate = observed bad-event fraction / 0.001. That arithmetic does not turn missing observations into good events.

Use sustained customer-impact burn to page, supported by short and long windows; lower urgency degradation creates owned follow-up work. Exact thresholds and windows require traffic-volume testing and on-call agreement. Low-volume tenants need synthetic probes and explicit counts, while P1 missing-work and P3 correctness violations require direct handling even when aggregate burn is low. Google SRE's alerting guidance explains the burn-rate approach.

Product and managers approve D, F, eligibility, recovery windows and costs through architecture and prioritization review. Baselines inform feasibility; customer needs determine targets. Record target versions and apply denominator changes prospectively, never to erase a breach. Implementing SLOs provides the underlying good-events/total-events model.

Turn metrics into decisions

Three views: operations gets current impact, burn, oldest work and dependency saturation; team review gets P1–P3 invariants, cohort trends and fault-test results; Product/Business gets affected tenants, timely deliveries, support burden and cost. Every view shows coverage and last update time.

Decision record: state the customer hypothesis, baseline and data quality, alternative options, expected metric change, cost, accountable owner, pass/stop conditions and review date. For P2, for example, delivery retry isolation should reduce enrichment attempts without worsening ingestion, healthy-tenant delivery latency or loss accounting. Extra throughput alone cannot establish that result.

Verify: compare canary and control cohorts with similar load and endpoint behavior through peak traffic and a complete retry/recovery cycle. Reconcile accepted IDs and include pending outcomes. Stop for missing work, identity/status corruption, customer-objective regression or unsafe headroom. Expand only after service owners and SRE review evidence against the production verification gates; Product confirms the business tradeoff.
