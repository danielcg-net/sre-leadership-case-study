1.3 / Failure Priorities

Failure modes and reliability priorities

Prioritize irreversible loss first, shared availability failures second, and duplicate external side effects third. This ranks architectural risk against the scenario's low tolerance for event loss, seconds-level delivery expectation, and 99.9% availability target. Incident frequency and commercial impact have not been measured.

Priority 1 · Durability

Accepted work loses its durable owner

Severity
Critical: missing accepted events may be unrecoverable, not merely late.
Blast radius
One event for an isolated crash; many tenants during a shared worker rollout, storage incident, or failed handoff path.
Frequency
Unknown. Every receipt acknowledgment exposes the assumed window; actual loss requires a crash or handoff failure within it.
Business impact
Missed customer workflows, manual reconciliation, and broken trust in acceptance and delivery status.

Detect

Reconcile unique accepted event IDs against durable pending work, confirmed endpoint acceptance, or an explicit needs-attention state. Alert on unaccounted IDs beyond a defined processing grace period, stale handoffs, and oldest pending age. Use an independent synthetic sender's acceptance ledger so a missing internal record cannot hide loss. Queue depth alone cannot detect vanished work.

Make resilient

Acknowledge ingestion only after durable append. Keep processing work replayable until the result and delivery intent commit atomically and the retained indexing prerequisite is satisfied. Dispatch directly from ready intents, tolerate repeated delivery with stable IDs, and recover abandoned claims with leases and reconciliation. This applies Guaranteed Delivery, Transactional Outbox and Idempotent Receiver; storage transactions and recovery retention are prerequisites.

Verify in production

For dedicated synthetic events, restart isolated workers after receipt, after result/intent commit, and after readiness but before input acknowledgment. Reconcile every accepted ID after the recovery deadline. Pass only if none is unaccounted for, each has a durable next action or terminal outcome, and the control endpoint records the expected effect. Observe through a restart, backlog drain and replay cycle. Any missing ID stops rollout; preserve pending intents and replay compatibility during rollback.

Priority 2 · Availability and latency

Enrichment degradation becomes a shared retry storm

Severity
High: widespread delivery delay can exhaust buffering and turn into loss or ingestion rejection.
Blast radius
Potentially all tenants sharing processing, enrichment, storage, or retry capacity.
Frequency
Enrichment degradation is the incident given in the scenario. Recurrence is unknown; the retry loop amplifies every failed delivery that re-enters processing.
Business impact
Time-sensitive integrations miss their deadlines, healthy tenants suffer collateral delay, and support demand grows.

Detect

Track accepted-to-endpoint-acceptance age, including pending events, by tenant cohort. Correlate oldest backlog age and drain rate with enrichment latency/timeouts, in-flight saturation, and processing attempts per unique event. Watch ingestion rejection and dashboard latency as containment guardrails. Page on sustained customer-impact/error-budget burn; successful-delivery percentiles alone omit stuck events.

Make resilient

Separate delivery retries from enrichment and reuse the stored payload. Bound enrichment duration and concurrency; use circuit breakers, per-tenant/endpoint capacity budgets, and delayed retries with jitter, maximum attempts and age. Route permanent failures or exhausted budgets to owned recovery. Monitor buffer headroom and retain existing ingress limits. Asynchronous search projection and broader admission redesign are future scope; index completion remains a readiness gate. Retry, Circuit Breaker, Bulkhead and Dead Letter Channel provide the relevant controls.

Verify in production

Introduce latency and endpoint rejection only for a dedicated test tenant through a scoped fault profile. Compare canary and control cohorts at matched traffic: delivery retries must add no enrichment calls, attempt counts stay within budget, unaffected tenants remain within their delivery objective, and backlog drains within the agreed recovery window. Check peak traffic and an entire retry-budget cycle. Stop on faster error-budget burn, falling storage headroom, or regression in ingestion/dashboard availability.

Priority 3 · Delivery correctness

An ambiguous webhook outcome repeats a customer side effect

Severity
High; potentially critical for customer operations that cannot safely be repeated.
Blast radius
Usually one event or endpoint; a shared network failure or replay can affect many tenants.
Frequency
Unknown. Timeouts, lost responses and worker crashes are credible triggers; no duplicate incidence is supplied.
Business impact
Duplicate business actions, inconsistent records, and costly customer investigation or reversal.

Detect

Record stable tenant/event/delivery IDs, payload version, attempt ID, endpoint outcome and status version. Detect retries after ambiguous responses, changed payloads across delivery attempts, and status regression. Count duplicate customer effects only where receipt evidence exists: platform attempts alone cannot prove what an uncontrolled endpoint executed.

Make resilient

Reuse an immutable payload and a stable delivery key; make platform writes idempotent and reject stale status updates. Persist outcomes and next actions before releasing work, then reconcile incomplete records. Define recipient deduplication and retention in the webhook contract. Expose unknown outcomes and controlled replay; Idempotent Receiver and Message History cannot enforce customer cooperation or promise exactly-once external execution.

Verify in production

A controlled receiver commits a counted side effect, then drops its response. Also restart the test worker before outcome persistence, and deliver an older failure update after success. Pass if repeated requests keep the same key and payload, the deduplicating receiver records one effect, and dashboard/API status does not regress and converges within the freshness objective. Repeat with a receiver that does not deduplicate to expose the contractual limit. Stop on key/payload drift, false success, or duplicate effects at the cooperating receiver.

Production verification discipline

Before rollout, baseline loss reconciliation, delivery age, retry amplification and status lag across a representative peak and retry cycle. Agree numeric delivery, recovery and freshness limits; the scenario supplies a 99.9% availability target but no complete latency or freshness SLO. Measure ingestion by valid submissions durably accepted, and delivery by unique accepted events reaching endpoint acceptance within the agreed deadline; overdue pending events count as misses, not missing samples. Define availability separately from latency. Keep customer-endpoint failures visible and separately attributed instead of silently excluding them from reported outcomes.

Run fault experiments on synthetic tenants and controlled receivers through the production path with isolated resources and a kill switch. Never induce failures at real customer endpoints. Then expand a small real-traffic canary only after its pre-agreed gates pass; compare with a concurrent control and retain a rollback path that preserves outstanding work. Record event counts, observation duration, before/after distributions and exceptions. Zero observed loss in a bounded experiment is evidence, not proof of impossibility.

Processing owners validate acknowledgment and enrichment controls; delivery owners validate retry and receipt behavior; data owners validate persistence and projections. SRE coordinates experiments, customer-impact measures and rollout decisions with those teams.
