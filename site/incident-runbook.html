5.1 / Runbook-Led Response

Protect customers, coordinate owners, verify recovery

At minute 15: enrichment is degraded, processing is stalling, and webhook delays and failures are spreading as the backlog grows. The dependency has already been identified. The response now focuses on containment, recovery and communication.

Both response models use the same objectives: preserve accepted work, protect unaffected customers, restore timely delivery, communicate impact, and reconcile the backlog. Automated monitoring, paging and established scaling belong in the runbook-led model too.

Presentation plan: one minute for objectives and command; four for this response; three for AI assistance; two for maturity and validation.

Command without line authority

The Incident Commander coordinates priorities and decisions; an Operations Lead coordinates changes with service owners; a Communications Lead manages internal and external updates. SRE can assume command under a pre-agreed incident mandate without managing the teams. Each service owner retains responsibility for its production actions. Keep one incident channel and a timestamped decision record. This separation follows established incident-management practice.

Page Processing, Delivery and the actual enrichment owner now; engage Ingestion for admission and buffer protection. Assign the roles immediately if not already established. The architecture consensus board is not an incident-time approval gate.

Operating agreement: these severity definitions and timing targets are proposals, not existing policy. By minute 15, check for missed detection, paging and communication deadlines and act immediately; do not restart those clocks when the cause becomes known.

Respect the current architecture during mitigation

Scaling Processing can send more concurrent calls into degraded enrichment and worsen the incident. The existing delivery retry path also repeats processing. Use only controls the service owners can safely operate now; the proposed durable-intent architecture is not assumed deployed.

Under the current acknowledgment-on-receipt assumption, pausing or restarting workers may lose in-memory work. Establish where each accepted event remains recoverable before changing consumption or terminating workers. Do not bypass enrichment, introduce an untested circuit breaker, or claim safe replay without an established recovery contract. If no safe control exists, escalate that constraint explicitly and prioritize retaining accepted work.

Customer update

Webhook delivery is delayed for some customers. We are reducing pressure on an internal dependency and monitoring recovery. We are validating the status of pending deliveries. Next update in 15 minutes.

Use this wording only once the stated mitigation is underway. Include confirmed scope and timestamp where available. Do not promise a recovery time or “no data loss” before evidence supports it. Maintain one factual update for status-page and account communications.
